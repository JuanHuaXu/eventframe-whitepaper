# Observation attention extension review

Date: 2026-09-12. Scope: Section 2 orientation, Section 13 X3, claim ledgers,
glossary, outline, and source attribution. Existing exploratory edits preserved.

## Source and interpretation

The complete English automatic captions of Surnex's 10:29 video were retrieved
and read. Metadata gives publication date 2026-09-01. The source motivates two
axes, temporal scope and depth, not neural attention. It is attributed as an
informal conceptual account rather than evidence of a cognitive theorem.
No full transcript is redistributed. EventFrame's controller is our adaptation.

## Mathematical and integration review

- The new controller adds no alternative scored law or incompatible outcome space.
  Scope changes cannot silently change the horizon of a prior scored forecast.
- Task-relative scopes are not identical to fast/slow execution paths. The same
  observation's appearance at several scopes retains a single evidence lineage.
- Why/how hypotheses and model-generated perturbations do not become causal or
  real-world evidence. Absence requires a horizon and observation-coverage model.
- Jobs carry immutable snapshots and dependency versions, bounded expansion and
  queue budgets, visited-state limits, explicit stopping, and stale invalidation.
- Cheap frontier-all updates remain intact. Selection-conditioned inference,
  independent audit coverage, poisoning protections, Anti-Pigeon authority, and
  atomic reversible publication are not bypassed by the controller.
- A plan is not authority to execute or acquire observations. Missing interfaces
  or models require an unresolved result rather than invented observations.

## Falsifiers and remaining gaps

Controls separate fixed-view loops, scope-only, depth-only, and joint scheduling.
Common-observation replay is distinct from paid active observation. Future
evaluation must split trajectories/lineages and measure loss, task success, harm,
coverage, starvation, and serving latency. More elaborate explanations alone do
not count as success. A fixed-token neural experiment tests neither this
controller nor its integration with event structure.

X3 remains unimplemented and Not tested in both ledgers. Selection rules and task
instantiations must be frozen in a later experimental protocol; no scheduler
optimality, learning guarantee, or latency improvement is claimed here.
