# Appendix C. Approximation Error and Prospective Inference

## C.1 What the Bound Compares

This appendix supplies a conditional exact-arithmetic theorem for the capped current-state filter, not a proof that its model is true. Fix one latent measurable state space, prior, likelihood family, chronological evidence history and predictive map. Let \(P_t^{\mathrm{full}}\) be the unrestricted filter and \(P_t^{\mathrm{cap}}\) the filter with declared component cap \(c_{\mathrm{cap}}\). The implementation may represent a component by its class, starting time and member-state probabilities; an observable joint law is a common measurable projection of that representation. Total variation is \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|\), with range [0,1]. Use \(\epsilon_t^{\mathrm{cap}}\) for a proved upper bound, not an empirical estimate of this distance.

At step \(t\), both filters use the same transition \(K_t^{\mathrm{reset}}=(1-\rho_t)K_t+\rho_t R_t\), where \(R_t\) is a state-independent reset law and \(0\le\rho_t\le1\). Both condition on the same observed likelihood \(g_t\), with declared bounds \(0\le m_t\le g_t\le M_t\), \(M_t>0\). Unavailable evidence has \(g_t=1\), not an invented observation. After conditioning, the capped filter retains support \(S_t\) and renormalizes; \(d_t(c_{\mathrm{cap}})\) bounds the discarded probability mass in that approximate, pre-pruning updated law. All supports, likelihoods and bounds must refer to the same as-of replay. Changing the model, refitting support or changing the observation policy requires a new comparison; a bound cannot be transferred merely because the cap count matches.

## C.2 Filtering and Truncation Theorem

Suppose \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{full}},P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{cap}})\le\epsilon_{t-1}^{\mathrm{cap}}\). Define the predicted error \(u_t=(1-\rho_t)\epsilon_{t-1}^{\mathrm{cap}}\). Let \(z_t\) be a justified lower bound on the capped pre-pruning evidence integral \(\int g_t\,dP_t^{\mathrm{cap},-}\). A lower bound on the unrestricted evidence integral is \(b_t^{\mathrm{ev}}=\max(m_t,z_t-(M_t-m_t)u_t)\). Both conditioning operations must exist. When \(b_t^{\mathrm{ev}}>0\), set \(v_t=\min(1,M_tu_t/b_t^{\mathrm{ev}})\); when that lower bound is nonpositive and \(u_t>0\), use \(v_t=1\). If \(u_t=0\) and the evidence is possible, use \(v_t=0\). Then \(\epsilon_t^{\mathrm{cap}}=\min(1,v_t+d_t(c_{\mathrm{cap}}))\) is a valid current-state TV upper bound.

*Proof.* A common Markov kernel cannot increase TV. Mixing with the same state-independent reset law multiplies the difference by \(1-\rho_t\). For probability measures \(P,Q\), any bounded real function \(f\) satisfies \(|\int f\,d(P-Q)|\le(\sup f-\inf f)d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)\). Applying this to \(g_t\) gives the stated evidence lower bound. For an arbitrary event \(A\), let \(a_A=Q^{g_t}(A)\). The normalized difference is \(P^{g_t}(A)-Q^{g_t}(A)=\int g_t(1_A-a_A)\,d(P-Q)/\int g_t\,dP\). The integrand's oscillation is at most \(M_t\), so the conditioning defect is bounded by \(M_tu_t/b_t^{\mathrm{ev}}\), clipped at one. Conditioning a probability measure on retained support of positive mass changes it in TV by exactly its removed mass. The triangle inequality proves the recurrence. No probability floor is used; an actually impossible observation remains inadmissible.

*Safe-cap corollary.* If \(m_t\ge m>0\), \(M_t\le M\), and \(\rho_t\ge\rho\), define \(a=(1-\rho)M/m\). If \(a<1\) and removed mass is uniformly at most \(\bar d(c_{\mathrm{cap}})\), then \(\epsilon_T^{\mathrm{cap}}\le a^T\epsilon_0^{\mathrm{cap}}+\bar d(c_{\mathrm{cap}})\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}a^j\). In particular, \(\epsilon_0^{\mathrm{cap}}\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{safe}}\) and \(\bar d(c_{\mathrm{cap}})\le(1-a)\epsilon_{\mathrm{safe}}\) imply \(\epsilon_t^{\mathrm{cap}}\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{safe}}\) for every step. With \(\rho=0.75,m=0.5,M=1\) and tolerance 0.01, discarded mass at most 0.005 per step is sufficient in exact arithmetic. This is a conditional region, not a claim that the current corpus satisfies it.

A component count alone supplies no small uniform removed-mass bound. A cap of one can omit a tiny prior alternative that dominates after rare contrary evidence: if its prior mass is \(\delta\), retained likelihood is \(\delta^2\) and omitted likelihood is one, the unrestricted omitted posterior mass tends to one as \(\delta\) tends to zero. With zero lower likelihood or \(a\ge1\), the uniform corollary may be unavailable; the evidence-aware per-step bound can still be useful, but it must be evaluated rather than assumed. The observed 0.698245997 joint TV is therefore a material failure of fidelity in that audited capped case, not an error repaired by log arithmetic. A valid upper envelope need not reproduce that measured distance exactly or make it small.

## C.3 Predictive Loss and Numerical Admission

For one common Markov predictive map \(F\), \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(PF,QF)\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)\). A Bernoulli forecast therefore has probability difference \(|p-q|\le\epsilon\). For the same outcome \(y\in\{0,1\}\), single-coordinate Brier obeys \(|(p-y)^2-(q-y)^2|=|p-q||p+q-2y|\le\min(1,2\epsilon)\). The same bound holds for expected loss under any common outcome law. It is not a log-loss bound, a calibration result, an external truth certificate, or a guarantee for two independently changed residual kernels or template maps. A joint TV near 0.7 can coexist with identical particular marginals; conversely its general Brier envelope is vacuous at one. Smaller bounds on the actual predictive projection may be more useful than a full latent-joint envelope.

Separate ideal capped inference from computed floating inference. Let \(\nu_t^{\mathrm{num}}\) be an independently justified upper budget covering the propagated difference between the ideal capped and computed laws, plus any underestimation introduced by computing the analytic envelope from rounded masses and evidence. An oracle-parity maximum over sampled inputs does not establish this uniform budget. Total admission uses \(\epsilon_t^{\mathrm{total}}=\min(1,\widehat\epsilon_t^{\mathrm{cap}}+\nu_t^{\mathrm{num}})\) and Brier-shift bound \(\min(1,2\epsilon_t^{\mathrm{total}})\). Admit only when both lie within frozen tolerances; absent, stale or unjustified numerical coverage requires a declared fallback or shadow-only output. Outward rounding of the bound calculation protects that calculation given valid inputs, not the upstream exponential, normalization and replay computations.

The reference research implementation adds evidence-aware propagation, a safe-discard calculator, outward-rounded scalar bound arithmetic and opt-in transactional issue admission. The latter checks the law after support selection and leaves history and epochs unchanged on rejection. Conditional clean forecasts for counterfactual branches require their own replay bounds; a current-state bound alone is not used to certify historical-query evidence-ratio weights. Those weights and their full historical path fidelity remain open. The new gate requires a named external numerical budget and does not synthesize one from audit parity. Legacy research outputs remain explicitly uncertified; no production default is silently changed.

## C.4 Prospective Statistical Contract

Historical fixed-sample screens keep their frozen acceptance rules. Their approximate normal intervals are not upgraded retroactively into finite-sample or anytime guarantees. Absence of a confidence sequence alone does not invalidate a genuinely fixed-sample test; small samples, distributional assumptions, multiplicity, exposed data and fitting-model reuse determine its evidential limits. The v3 comparison has 32 stream means per scenario/split conditional on one fit. V8 has 24 per case conditional on three fitted incumbents. Within-stream forecasts and multiple arms evaluated on the same stream are not independent sample units.

For a new paired Bernoulli-Brier comparison, freeze a completed-stream statistic \(X_j\in[-1,1]\), its estimand \(\mu\), model/fit grouping, a comparison-family size \(k_{\mathrm{cmp}}\), and family error budget \(\alpha_{\mathrm{CS}}\) before opening new outcomes. The reference confidence sequence assumes \(\mathbb E[X_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]=\mu\); this is not automatic for changes of generator or adaptively selected fitting models. Allocate \(\alpha_{j,c}=\alpha_{\mathrm{CS}}/[k_{\mathrm{cmp}}j(j+1)]\) to each time and comparison. Hoeffding gives half-width \(h_j=\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha_{j,c})/j}\) for range two. Intersect \([\bar X_j-h_j,\bar X_j+h_j]\) with [-1,1]. Summing the two-sided failure probabilities over every declared comparison and time proves simultaneous anytime coverage at least \(1-\alpha_{\mathrm{CS}}\). This is a real-arithmetic theorem; the float64 reference utility is formula-tested but does not claim formally certified interval rounding. This conservative reference is inexpensive, but variance-adaptive empirical-Bernstein or betting constructions can improve power under their respective assumptions [28,29]. No sequence makes an arbitrarily small effect resolvable from a fixed small dataset.

The v3 member-shift AP gain 0.000564 has approximate interval [-0.000375,0.001502]; the recurring AP contrast is instead -0.000348 [-0.001846,0.001149]. The first comparison's observed standard error is approximately 0.00026822. A normal planning approximation with the original 3.5 multiplier, 80% power, unchanged paired variance and assumed true gain 0.000564 yields about 137 fresh streams per comparison. This is a noisy pilot-based planning calculation, not a guaranteed requirement, a confidence-sequence stopping boundary, or evidence for an AP effect. A new design should freeze a scientifically meaningful minimum gain and fit-level replication rather than merely collect until the old point estimate turns significant.

The omitted-influence fixture similarly distinguishes coverage from usefulness: 256/256 sampled finite populations covered, but mean upper bound 0.21999 is 41.8 times mean actual influence 0.00526. Useful tightness at a frozen admission tolerance, not existence of a worst-case bound, remains an open formulation and experiment item. The current work does not label that certificate usable in deployment or claim exhaustive never-nominated coverage.

## C.5 Correction Evidence and Component Cost

The local correction records in `evidence/approximation-inference-v1/` pin candidate source by SHA-256, not a published commit. They reuse development fixtures and do not change historical acceptance gates. The capped-filter audit retained 193,613 checks, 48,780 summary-parity comparisons and 280 dense-envelope comparisons, of which 225 were nonvacuous. Its maximum measured joint defect stayed 0.698245997. Exact-rational finite tests additionally check the scalar recurrence and safe region. An independent review found and prompted repair of positive underflow in bound arithmetic and subnormal scale loss in the normal planner; those regressions now pass. These checks establish scoped implementation behavior, not the validity of a caller's external numerical witness or the true generating law.

Matched prepared issue/query benchmarks compare immutable control revision `7a7b935` with the local correction, at 2, 150 and 200 members and 32 or 64 rows. The source manifest records its complete commit identity. Six samples per profile/arm were run in alternating before-after/after-before order. Median changes across the twelve profiles were -1.92% to +0.47%, with unchanged allocation counts; these differences are descriptive timing variation, not established speedup or zero overhead. On the same M4, Go 1.27.1, the scalar bound cost 25.66--25.91 ns/op with zero allocations. Three-sample bounded-issue medians at 64 rows were 242.477, 527.521 and 620.865 microseconds for 2, 150 and 200 members, versus 246.992, 523.639 and 620.407 for legacy issue; transactional prefix copying added one allocation. The admission benchmark deliberately uses vacuous budgets of one and a synthetic zero numerical witness, measuring mechanics rather than acceptable fidelity. Longer retained prefix histories need their own cost measurement.

The stream-level inference utility likewise adds inexpensive bookkeeping: three serial update-and-bound measurements were 29.26--29.51 ns/op with zero allocations. A deterministic 4,096-stream utility fixture produced mean 0.1875 and interval [0.087880,0.287120]. This is an implementation check on authored paired Brier inputs, not scientific confirmation, an empirical coverage study or an attained-power result. The separate AP planning example retains 137 total fresh streams under its stated approximate assumptions.

The participant-extraction audit uses immutable control `1a7edb6`, whose complete commit identity is recorded in the frame benchmark report, and public synthetic text at 256 B, 2 KiB and 16 KiB per role. Three forward and three reverse repetitions cover five profiles. It found two semantic defects: masked captures could reintroduce quoted bytes as direct evidence, and capitalized collective pronouns could bypass fallback as named actors. The repaired capture rule rejects spans crossing masked bytes, and the actor rule rejects pronouns; raw metadata and summaries remain retained. Query/text and identity-enrichment controls cover adjacent paths. The stale multiline-quote test now requires abstention from direct mechanism extraction while retaining raw/summary text and excluding active quoted identity evidence.

This repair is not free: unquoted profile medians increased 4.54--29.26%, and quote-only profiles increased 138.00--186.26% relative to the unsafe baseline, also reflecting different fallback paths. Candidate medians ranged from 39.879 microseconds to 11.294 milliseconds across tested input sizes. Repeated quote masking still scales with supplied text and allocates. These are complete `FromTurn` component timings, not retrieval or loaded service latency. Earlier canonical-frame quality results do not validate this revised extractor; outcome replay, calibration compatibility and live agent validation remain open. No production installation or datastore was changed.
